# Review of where the project stands (2026-11-07)

The owner asked, once spec 005 was done, for a step back: where Oneframe Lab stands against its end
goal, and how well its way of working serves it. It follows the [review of 2026-11-04](2026-11-04.md)
and its structure, three days and five merged pull requests later.

One session on the owner's PC read the rules, the [handoff](../handoff.md),
[architecture.md](../architecture.md), the [roadmap](../roadmap.md), the earlier reviews, specs 005
and 015 to 017 with their tasks and reports, and the code that changed from `8e20a9b` (the last
review) to `67259b1` (`main`, with PR #61). It ran the checks and changed no code. The claims under
"Checked in the code" were confirmed directly. The rest cite a file and line, a commit or a PR, or
are marked as the reviewer's reasoning. The owner decides what is done.

## Verdict

**What went right since 2026-11-04:**

- **The order the owner set was kept.** The spike ran first: one day, all 13 settings on the 1070
  ([report](../../specs/005-first-models/reports/2026-11-03-spike.md)). Spec 005 was then cut to
  its facts, approved on 2026-11-05 and done two days later. No infrastructure spec started.
- **Real models run.** Depth Pro, MoGe-2 and MoGe-3 (without its refinement) load from the store,
  each family in its own runtime, on the card and on the processor. CI's `models (cpu)` job runs
  all three with their real weights on Windows and Ubuntu, in 6 to 7 minutes.
- **The hardware-report rule paid for itself.** AC7's benches showed the spike's figures were low
  where it matters. Depth Pro's fp16 peak on the card is torch's reserve, 1.18 times the estimate
  then in `main`, and its system memory 1.39 times. Both were refitted before anyone else ran the
  node ([AC7](../../specs/005-first-models/reports/2026-11-06-ac7.md)).
- **The small-fix path works.** Three of the seven PRs since then needed no spec (#56, #57, #62).
  Each was named by a review or a report, and each code fix came with its test.

**What is wrong:**

- **A person still can do nothing new.** `npm start` shows the page of 2026-10-04: `app/renderer/`
  has not changed since. First light is three specs away (015 to 017), all still drafts. The drafts
  lack both changes the last review asked for before their code, and one of them carries a wrong
  number (section 1).
- **The owner's queue is the bottleneck, and it is going stale.** The rules PR the owner was to
  read ([#59](https://github.com/cbgithub7/oneframe-lab/pull/59), open since 2026-11-04) now
  conflicts with `main`. Three Dependabot PRs are green and unmerged; one has waited since
  2026-10-24 and is already superseded. `main` is still unprotected.
- **What was decided without a spec was not done.** The handoff was to shrink to about 80 lines,
  and it grew to 133. `AGENTS.md` was to be trimmed, and it grew to 1,769 words. A decision that
  has no `tasks.md` has nothing to track it.
- **The checks cost seven minutes a turn on the owner's PC.** `npm run engine:check` took 396 s
  here today, 357 s of it pytest; the last review measured about 100 s. CI's whole Windows job takes
  2 minutes 15 s.
- **The first real nodes brought copies.** Three runtimes repeat one torch's builds and pins, and
  nothing holds them equal. The two MoGe nodes repeat one file. Nothing checks the runtimes' pins
  against their latest releases.

**The risk:** first light waits on the owner's reading, not on the code. Spec 005 shows that the
code side is fast once a spec is approved: two days. The order below clears the owner's queue
first, then makes the three drafts ready to approve as one decision.

### The open findings

- **MoGe's peak is the larger of two parts** (AC7, finding 1). The report calls this waste, never a
  failed run, but it also leads to wrong choices on small cards. The pixels term (123.2 MB a
  megapixel at every level) is added on top of a network part, while the real peak is the larger
  of the two parts, not their sum. So the gap grows with the photo, and the fit lowers the level
  (costing quality) where the real peak would fit.
    - An example, the reviewer's arithmetic from the fitted figures, not measured: take the test
      machines' 6 GB card, with 6,000 MB free, which leaves a budget of 4,389 MB after the margin.
      For a 16 MP photo, level 9's estimate is 4,753 MB, so the fit picks level 5 (4,320 MB). By the
      report's two parts, level 9's peak would be about 3.4 GB.
    - The memory model's language has no larger-of: it is "a base plus terms"
      (`engine/src/oneframe/memory/model.py:9-10`). The last review said to grow that language
      only from 005's numbers, and this is the one change they ask for.
    - It changes the manifest's format, which a person's own node will also write (017's
      `ONEFRAME_NODES`). Decision 4.
- **Depth Pro's fp32 figure on a card is still the spike's** (AC7, finding 4). It is allocated
  memory, where every other card figure is torch's reserve, which for Depth Pro at fp16 is 18 %
  more.
    - The estimate on a card is 9,225 MB: 3,813 MB of weights and 5,412 MB of working memory. With
      18 % on the whole, as at fp16, it would be about 10.9 GB (the reviewer's arithmetic).
    - The fit asks for the estimate plus the 1,611 MB margin free
      (`engine/src/oneframe/memory/settings.py:16`), 10.8 GB in all, so the margin absorbs the
      shortfall almost exactly. A card just over that line could run out of memory once, then learn
      its figure (`learned.json`).
    - No realistic card fails for this today. But the margin is doing the figure's work, so the
      roadmap's "derive the margin again" must wait until this figure is measured.
- **The handoff's Open list:**
    - *The three items for first light* are in 016 and 017 (above).
    - *MoGe-3's refinement* needs compute 8.0 or newer and FlexGEMM, whose wheels are for Linux
      only. On the owner's RTX 5080 under Windows that means WSL, the Improve-loop spike's own

## 2. Code

This section reads what changed since `8e20a9b` for drift: `bench_fit.py`, `runtime_check.py`,
the three nodes, the two runtimes, `run_on_cpu.py`, the workflow, `check-versions.js` and the hooks.

What holds:

- **"Light engine":** `server.py` did not change, and the light-import test passes.
- **"Nothing hard-coded":** no engine or app code names the new nodes or runtimes. `run_on_cpu.py`
  and the CI job find them by looking. The card's name appears only in the manifests' `_sources`,
  as 005's requirement 9 asks.
- **UTF-8 on every text open** in the new code.
- **"Honest labels":** every model output is `predicted`.

The findings:

1. **One torch, three copies** (against "one way to do each thing").
    - `depth_pro` and `moge` repeat the torch runtime's build table (lines in "Checked in the
      code"), its `torch==2.14.0` extras (`runtimes/depth_pro/pyproject.toml:14-16,21`,
      `runtimes/moge/pyproject.toml:17-19`) and its three indexes. Their `_sources` say "as
      runtimes/torch's".
    - A new torch, a card it drops or a new CUDA build means three edits and three locks, and a
      missed edit fails nothing. Each new family adds another copy.
    - *Now, small:* a test that each runtime's builds and torch pin equal `runtimes/torch`'s, unless
      `versions.json` gives a reason. The rule "Versions" allows an older torch line for old cards.
2. **The runtimes' pins are checked by no tool.** Neither `npm run versions` nor Dependabot reads a
   runtime (both above). So "a runtime pins the newest release its family's code and wheels
   support" (`AGENTS.md:78-82`) is now held by hand, for every direct pin of three runtimes. *Before
   step 4 adds more:* `npm run versions` reads each runtime's direct pins, on the same terms as the
   engine's.
3. **A graph walk outside the graph module.** `upstream_of` and `part_of`
   (`engine/src/oneframe/bench_fit.py:229-248`) walk the graph's edges by hand. 015 needs the same
   walk twice: to find a cycle for `fits`, and the steps that depend on a failed one (its
   requirement 6). *In 015:* one walk in `graph.py`, which the bench uses too.
4. **Two MoGe nodes, one file.** `nodes/depth.moge2/node.py` and `nodes/depth.moge3/node.py`
   differ only in `TITLE`, `CONFIG`, `INFER`, `META` and the `MoGeModel` import. A fix to one is
   a fix to both, by hand. Spec 005 chose two nodes for their outputs and ports (Decisions);
   nothing yet lets two nodes share code. *When a third MoGe node comes* (ViT-G, say): put the shared part in the
   runtime's folder, on the child's path as its sources and stand-ins are, or say in a spec why
   copies are better.
5. **Small things, for when the files are next touched:**
    - `engine/src/oneframe/runtime_check.py:166` hands pyright `dict(os.environ)`, the one child
      environment in `engine/src` not built by `environment.py`;
    - `engine/tests/models/run_on_cpu.py:42-44` hashes the pins' sha256s by hand, a fifth sha256
      beside the four the last review listed, which are still there;
    - `run_on_cpu.py:47-58` finds the photo node by its shape, so a second node of that shape would
      stop CI's models job (016 changes this code anyway).
